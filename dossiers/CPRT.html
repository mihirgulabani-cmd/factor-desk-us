Step 1 — What it is & the numbers

  • Salvage-auction duopolist (~50% US share): consignment model, owned land, global buyer network. FY2026 (Jul-end, reported 10 Sep): revenue $4.7B (+0.4%), NI $1.48B (−4.4%), EPS $1.55 (−2.5%) — first decline in years. Q4 EPS missed ~10%; op income −10.6%.
  • Volume leak: global insurance assignments −7.5% (−2.3% ex one lost major carrier); US earned car years −4% as consumers drop coverage; collision frequency −3.4% YoY (ADAS). Offsets: ASPs +5.5%, international units +10%, total-loss frequency at record 23.1%.
  • Regime breaks: surprise CEO swap (Liaw out, founder Adair back — stock −8%), $1.9B all-cash ACV Auctions tender (dilutive until FY28), first big buybacks ($1.6B).

Step 2 — Competitive position

  • RB Global (IAA): SIX straight quarters of share gains, automotive GTV +13%, expanded its largest insurance partner to all 50 states — priced BELOW Copart (take rate −110bps). Progressive's mix shifted to ~90% IAA per channel checks.
  • OPENLANE +15% (whole-car, adjacent); ACV being absorbed by Copart itself.

Step 3 — The short-seller case

  • Structural volume impairment: ADAS compounding on accident frequency; total-loss frequency at 23%+ is near a mathematical plateau — the decade's offset is spent.
  • Duopoly discipline broken: insurers demonstrably willing to re-tender; a full carrier walked.
  • EV totals economics (fire/storage costs, weak battery salvage bids) unproven.
  • 17× forward on NEGATIVE EPS growth is not cheap; the 34× median multiple was earned by ~20% compounding that no longer exists. Director sold $2.2M in July.

Step 5 — Decision record

Verdict: Franchise intact (land, buyers, record ASPs), growth algorithm broken (volumes structurally leaking, real share losses to a re-energized IAA).
